50% and 45 seconds on the page

Slide-in, once per session

The prompt needs both conditions. Scrolling fast past halfway is skimming, and 45 seconds at the top of the page is a tab left open, so it appears only for a reader who has done both.

End of article

Inline form, already shipped

Stays where it is, above "Continue reading". The slide-in never appears once someone has reached it, so the same person is not asked twice.

Desktop only

Exit card, once per visitor

When the pointer leaves through the top of the window and the slide-in has not been shown or dismissed. A modal does not interrupt reading at that moment, because the reader is already leaving. Phones have no equivalent, so they never get this one.

Rules

What makes this safe for search and for the people reading

Ship

  • Once per session, then a 30-day memory. "Don't show again" and a successful signup both end it for good.
  • Both conditions to appear: 50% depth and 45 seconds, so skimming and a forgotten tab both stay quiet.
  • Non-modal on phones and in the desktop gutter. Only the exit card is a dialog, and it traps focus properly and closes on Escape.
  • Signed-in readers never see the résumé offer. Its footnote promises a sign-in step that would be false for them, so they get the email card or nothing.
  • Reduced motion removes the entrance outright, as the rest of the marketing surface does. It simply appears.
  • Every number comes from a constant. The only figures near this card are in the article itself, which already cites its source.
  • Events through the typed analytics seam: shown, dismissed, submitted, each with post, variant and trigger, so the result can be read instead of guessed.

Never

  • Anything on arrival from search. A full-screen overlay on the first screen of a mobile search visit is the pattern Google's interstitial guidance targets, and these posts are the best door the site has.
  • A timer on its own. Time on the page without scroll depth is usually a tab left open.
  • A scrim or scroll lock on a phone.
  • Guilt copy or a countdown. "No thanks, I don't want to get hired" is the tone that loses people who would have come back.
  • Re-showing after a dismissal within 30 days.
  • Covering the table of contents, the byline or the existing end-of-article form.
